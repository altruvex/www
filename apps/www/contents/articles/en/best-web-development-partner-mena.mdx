Most companies start by asking "who is the best web development company?" The more useful question is "what kind of partner does this project need, and which candidates can prove they deliver it?" This guide works through both.

There is no universally best option. A freelancer solves a skills gap; a studio or agency solves a coordination problem across several disciplines. Pick the type before you compare names.

## Seven criteria for choosing a web development company in MENA

### 1. Arabic and English built as equals

If your customers or staff read Arabic, the Arabic site cannot be a translation added at the end.

Ask to see a live bilingual site the candidate built, switch languages on your phone, and check whether the Arabic layout reads naturally right to left, uses a proper Arabic typeface, and has its own titles and descriptions for search. Our article on [bilingual architecture](/writing/multilingual-architecture) lists what to look for.

### 2. Local integrations

Regional payment providers, bank transfer flows, delivery and logistics services, and SMS gateways each have their own requirements. A good partner asks about them in discovery, not at launch, and connects them through their own APIs rather than a generic plugin. Ask which regional providers the candidate has integrated and what they needed from the client.

### 3. Data protection awareness

Several countries in the region now have comprehensive personal data laws:

- **Saudi Arabia**: the Personal Data Protection Law came into force in September 2023, with enforcement from September 2024 ([overview](https://securiti.ai/saudi-arabia-personal-data-protection-law/)).
- **United Arab Emirates**: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 came into force in January 2022 ([UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/digital-uae/data/data-protection-laws)).
- **Egypt**: Law No. 151 of 2020 gained its executive regulations by Prime Ministerial Decree No. 816 of 2025, with a one-year transition for compliance ([overview](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/01/egypt-important-data-protection-update)).

A developer is not your lawyer, and you should take legal advice for your own obligations. But a good partner will ask what personal data the site collects, where it is stored, and how consent and deletion requests are handled, because those answers shape the architecture.

### 4. Ownership of code and accounts

Ask who owns the source code, domain, hosting and accounts, and from what point. Ask where the repository will live and what documentation you get at handover. If the answer is "we host it for you" with no transfer date, **you are renting your own website**.

### 5. A written process

You should be able to see, before signing, the phases of the work, what you approve at each one, how many revision rounds are included, and how changes are priced. Verbal assurances do not survive the first disagreement.

### 6. Performance on real devices and networks

Many of your visitors will be on mobile networks. Ask which performance thresholds the candidate holds to and how they check them on each release. Google's [Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) are a common, measurable reference.

### 7. Support after launch

Ask what a warranty covers, how long it lasts, what maintenance includes and excludes, and how quickly they reply. A partner who disappears at launch leaves you paying someone else to learn the system.

## A shortlisting process

1. **Write a one-page brief**: the outcome, users, languages, what exists, must-haves, deadline and a budget range.
2. **Choose the partner type** using the table above.
3. **Find three to five candidates** whose live work matches your type of project.
4. **Send everyone the same questions.** Our list of [questions to ask before hiring a web developer](/writing/evaluating-developers) is a starting point.
5. **Inspect live work** on your own phone, in both languages.
6. **Compare proposals by what they include**, not only the total: discovery, revisions, handover, warranty.
7. **Start small if unsure**: a paid discovery or [technical audit](/writing/technical-audit-before-rebuild) shows how a partner works before the full commitment.

## Choosing a web development company: common questions

### Should I hire a local company or work remotely?

What matters is the language, the integrations and the time zone overlap, not the office address. A remote partner who knows your market's languages and payment providers can serve you as well as one down the street.

### How long does a custom website take?

It depends on scope. Our [process page](/process) shows how long each of the five phases takes, and the development phase is sized to your project.

### How much does a web development company charge?

It depends on scope, integrations and languages. We publish our ranges on the [pricing page](/pricing) and show how an estimate is calculated on the [transparency page](/transparency).

### Do I need Arabic SEO separately?

Yes. Arabic searchers use different terms from a literal translation of your English keywords. Each language needs its own keyword research, titles and descriptions.

A · Findings register

The order is the product.

Anyone can hand you a list of problems. The audit hands you the sequence — what to fix first, what can wait, and why. Three passes over the same eighteen checks: the raw order they come out of the system in, the same set ranked worst-first, and the order we would actually work them.

RankWhat is examinedLeverage · risk / effort

    Honest by construction: these are the check classes a technical audit covers, not findings invented for a client who does not exist. The bar is leverage — risk removed per unit of effort — so only one of the three orders makes it fall as a clean staircase. That is the argument, drawn rather than claimed.

    B · Cost curve

    The same decision, priced four times.

    A wrong architecture costs almost nothing to change while it is still a sentence, and almost everything once it is live. The audit is a flat line drawn across that curve at the only point where it is still cheap.

    12,000 EGP is 4% of the largest build we publish, it happens before you commit to any of it, and it comes back off the build if you go ahead.

    12,000EGP, fixed. The audit is quoted, never estimated.
    5business days from access and context.
    22k–275kthe published build range the decision sits in front of.
    0net cost if you go on to build it with us — the 12,000 is credited against the project.

    Only the drawn numbers are claims: 12,000 and 5 days come from the pricing schema, 22,000–275,000 is the published matrix. The curve itself is labelled as shape, with no y-axis figures — we have not measured your system yet, and a chart that pretends otherwise is the thing this page exists to argue against.
